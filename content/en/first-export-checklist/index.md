A practical, roughly-ordered checklist for a first-time exporter. Not every item applies to every
product — treat it as a starting point, not a strict sequence.

## 1. Get set up to export legally

- [ ] Register for your **IEC** (Importer-Exporter Code) with DGFT. See the [IEC guide](/learn/iec).
- [ ] Confirm your **GST** registration covers exports, and understand the LUT (Letter of
      Undertaking) route for exporting without paying IGST upfront — ask your accountant if you
      are not sure this applies to you.
- [ ] Register with your product's **Export Promotion Council** for an RCMC (Registration-cum-
      Membership Certificate) — see the [Trade Connect and EPCs guide](/learn/trade-connect-and-epcs)
      to find the council for your product.
- [ ] Open an **AD (Authorised Dealer) code** registration with your bank's export division —
      your bank needs this to process incoming export payments against your shipments.

## 2. Know your product on paper

- [ ] Confirm your product's 8-digit **ITC-HS code** and export policy. See the
      [HS and ITC-HS guide](/learn/hs-and-itc-hs), or use the HS helper on this site.
- [ ] Check whether your product needs any additional certification (food safety, quality,
      destination-country standards) before it can be exported or imported at the other end.
- [ ] Decide your **MOQ** and, if useful, a smaller sample-order tier. See the [MOQ guide](/learn/moq).

## 3. Find and check buyers

- [ ] Search for buyers for your product and market on this site.
- [ ] For any new contact, run **Check a buyer** before you go further — before sharing detailed
      pricing, and definitely before sending a sample or agreeing on payment.
- [ ] Read the [scam red-flags guide](/scam-red-flags) so you recognise the common warning signs
      yourself, not only when this site flags them.

## 4. Reach out

- [ ] Write a short, specific first email. See the
      [guide to writing your first email](/learn/first-email).
- [ ] Decide your quoting Incoterm (most first-time exporters start with **FOB**). See the
      [Incoterms guide](/learn/incoterms).
- [ ] Have a plan for sample requests before you get one. See the
      [guide to handling sample requests](/learn/sample-requests).

## 5. Before you ship

- [ ] Agree payment terms in writing (advance payment share, letter of credit, or another method)
      before production starts.
- [ ] Confirm packaging and labelling requirements for the destination country.
- [ ] Arrange your shipping documents: commercial invoice, packing list, shipping
      bill/bill of lading or airway bill, and a certificate of origin if the buyer or destination
      country requires one.
- [ ] Confirm who arranges and pays for freight and insurance, based on the Incoterm you agreed.

*This checklist is general guidance for first-time exporters, not legal, tax or customs advice.
Requirements vary by product and destination — confirm the specifics that apply to you with your
customs broker (CHA), accountant, and relevant export promotion council.*
