## What it is

The **Importer-Exporter Code (IEC)** is a 10-digit code issued by the Directorate General of
Foreign Trade (DGFT). It is the single registration every Indian business needs before it can
legally import or export goods or services. Without a valid IEC, customs will not let a shipment
leave India in your name, and most banks will not process an export payment for you.

## Who needs one

Any proprietorship, partnership, LLP, private limited company or other legal entity that wants to
export from India. It is a one-time registration per PAN — you do not renew it shipment by
shipment, although DGFT does require an annual "update" confirmation (even a nil update) to keep
it active, or it can be deactivated.

## How to get one

IEC applications are made online through the DGFT website against your business PAN, with basic
KYC and bank account details. There is no fixed office visit required for most applicants — it is
designed to be a fast, largely automatic online process.

## Where it shows up in your outreach

Once you have an IEC, it is good practice to mention it in your business profile and in outreach
emails to buyers, alongside your business name and location. It is a simple, low-effort signal
that you are a properly registered exporter, not an unregistered trader.

## What it does *not* do

An IEC only makes exporting legally possible — it is not itself an endorsement of your product
quality, your factory, or your reliability as a supplier. Buyers will still want to see your
product, your samples, your certifications where relevant, and your track record.

*This page is general information, not legal or tax advice. Confirm current DGFT requirements
before you apply.*
