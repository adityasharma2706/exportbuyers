A sample request is usually a good sign — it means the buyer is seriously evaluating your
product, not just collecting quotes. It is also, unfortunately, the step scammers most often
abuse to get free goods with no intention of ever placing an order. Handling it well protects you
either way.

## Reasonable, normal requests

- A small number of units or a short length of material, enough to check quality, fit or
  finish — not enough to resell.
- A request paired with real conversation about specification, quantity, pricing or timeline for
  a bulk order. That context is what makes a sample request look like genuine sourcing.
- A willingness to pay for the sample and/or the courier cost, or at least to discuss it. Many
  legitimate buyers expect to pay for samples, especially from a new supplier they have not
  ordered from before.

## Warning signs to watch for

- **Free samples requested with no discussion of an order at all** — no quantity, no target
  price, no timeline, sometimes even before basic questions about your product are asked. See the
  [scam red-flags guide](/scam-red-flags) for this and other patterns.
  - Multiple "buyers" requesting free samples in a short time from the same message template.
- Pressure to ship the sample by an expensive courier (which you pay for) to an address that does
  not match the company's stated country or website.
- A buyer who goes quiet as soon as you ask for a courier account number, a nominal sample fee, or
  basic company details.

## A reasonable process

1. Ask a few qualifying questions before you agree to a sample: target quantity, target price
   range, and rough timeline. A genuine buyer can usually answer at least one of these.
2. Where practical, ask the buyer to use their own courier account (their DHL/FedEx account
   number) or to pay the courier cost. This is standard international trade practice and a real
   buyer will not be surprised by it.
3. Keep sample quantities small — enough to evaluate, not enough to be a free shipment.
4. Before you send anything, run the buyer through **Check a buyer** on this site. It is quick,
   free within your monthly allowance, and flags several of the patterns above automatically.

*This is general guidance based on common patterns, not a guarantee that following it prevents
every loss. Use your own judgement alongside it, especially for higher-value samples.*
