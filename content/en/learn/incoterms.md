## What Incoterms solve

When you quote a buyer a price, "the price" is meaningless on its own until you both agree who
pays for freight, who pays for insurance, and at what point risk of loss or damage passes from
you to the buyer. **Incoterms** (International Commercial Terms), published by the International
Chamber of Commerce, are a fixed set of three-letter terms that answer all three questions in one
word. Both sides agreeing on an Incoterm avoids a whole category of disputes later.

## The terms a first-time exporter meets most often

- **EXW (Ex Works)** — the buyer collects the goods from your factory or warehouse and arranges
  and pays for everything from there, including export customs clearance in India. Simplest for
  you, but buyers often prefer more support than this.
- **FOB (Free On Board)** — you arrange and pay for transport to the Indian port, customs
  clearance, and loading the goods onto the vessel. Once the goods are on board, cost and risk
  pass to the buyer, who arranges and pays for the sea freight and marine insurance onward. FOB
  is the most common term for first-time exporters selling by sea.
- **CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight)** — like FOB, but you also arrange and pay for the sea
  freight and marine insurance all the way to the buyer's destination port. Risk still passes to
  the buyer once the goods are loaded, but you are the one buying the freight and insurance.
- **DAP / DDP (Delivered at Place / Delivered Duty Paid)** — you take responsibility all the way
  to the buyer's door (DDP even covers the buyer's import duty). These give the buyer the least to
  do, but expose you to more cost and more places where something can go wrong before you are paid.

## Picking a term as a beginner

Most first-time Indian exporters start by quoting **FOB**, because your responsibility ends once
the goods are loaded at an Indian port that you know well, and the buyer arranges the parts of the
journey (ocean freight, destination customs) that they usually understand better than you do at
first. As you gain experience and relationships with reliable freight forwarders, CIF or DAP
quotes can make you a more attractive, lower-hassle supplier to buyers who would rather not
arrange freight themselves.

Whichever term you use, always write it next to your price: "USD 4.20/unit FOB Nhava Sheva" is
unambiguous; "USD 4.20/unit" on its own is not.

*This page is general information, not legal advice. For a specific shipment, confirm the term
and its consequences with your freight forwarder or customs broker.*
