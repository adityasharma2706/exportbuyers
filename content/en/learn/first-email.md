A cold outreach email to an overseas buyer has one job: earn a reply. That means it should be
short, specific, and easy to act on — not a full company brochure.

## What to include

1. **Who you are, in one line.** Your business name, city, and what you make. "We are a
   knitwear manufacturer based in Tiruppur, India" tells a buyer more in eight words than a
   paragraph of adjectives.
2. **Why you are writing to them specifically.** A line that shows you looked at their business —
   the product category they seem to import, or the market they sell into — rather than a
   generic blast. This is the single biggest difference between an email that gets read and one
   that gets deleted.
3. **One or two concrete facts about your product.** Certifications you hold, your usual MOQ,
   your main export markets so far, or your production capacity. Pick the two that are most
   relevant to this buyer, not every fact you have.
4. **A clear, low-effort next step.** Ask a specific question ("Would a sample set of 3 designs
   be useful?") rather than a vague "let me know if interested." A question is easier to answer
   than an open invitation.
5. **Your registration details**, briefly: IEC, and your business name and location, so the email
   reads as coming from a real, registered exporter.
6. **An opt-out line.** Buyers in many regions expect to be able to say "no, stop emailing me,"
   and offering it upfront is good practice everywhere, not just where it is legally required.

## What to leave out

- Do not claim your buyers, contacts, product, or business are "verified" or "genuine" as if a
  third party certified them — say what you actually know and can support (certifications you
  hold, markets you have shipped to), and let the buyer form their own judgement.
- Do not promise an order, a price, or a delivery date before you know what the buyer actually
  needs.
- Do not attach large files to a first email. A short email with a link to photos, or an offer to
  send a catalogue on request, gets read faster than one with a 10 MB PDF attached.

## Length

Aim for 120–180 words. If you cannot say it in that space, it belongs in a follow-up or an
attachment the buyer can choose to open, not the first email.

## Timing and follow-ups

If a buyer does not reply, one or two polite follow-ups spaced roughly a week apart is normal and
expected — most replies to cold outreach come from a follow-up, not the first email. After that,
move on; repeated unanswered emails read as pressure, not persistence.
