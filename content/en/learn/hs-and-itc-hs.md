## What an HS code is

The Harmonized System (HS) is a six-digit numbering system, maintained by the World Customs
Organization, that classifies every physical product traded internationally. Customs
authorities in almost every country use the same six digits for the same product, which is why
an HS code is the common language between your invoice, your shipping line, and the buyer's
customs broker.

The six digits get more specific as you read them:

- **Chapter (2 digits)** — a broad category, for example Chapter 61, "Articles of apparel and
  clothing accessories, knitted or crocheted".
- **Heading (4 digits)** — a narrower group within the chapter.
- **Subheading (6 digits)** — the internationally-agreed HS code itself.

## What ITC-HS adds

India extends the six-digit HS code to eight digits. This eight-digit version is called the
**ITC-HS code** (Indian Trade Classification, based on the Harmonized System), and it is
published and maintained by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT). The extra two
digits are India-specific and matter a great deal, because your product's **export policy** —
whether it is free to export, restricted (needs a licence or meets conditions), prohibited, or
reserved for a State Trading Enterprise — is decided at the 8-digit ITC-HS level, not the 6-digit
HS level.

Two products can share the same 6-digit HS subheading and still have different export policies
once you look at the 8th digit.

## Why it matters before you export

- Your export documents (shipping bill, invoice) must quote the correct 8-digit ITC-HS code.
- Getting it wrong can delay customs clearance, or in the worst case attract a penalty for
  misdeclaration.
- Many benefits (duty drawback rates, RoDTEP rates, EPC membership categories) are keyed to the
  HS code, so an inaccurate code can mean you miss benefits you were entitled to.

## How to find your code

Use the HS helper on this site: describe your product in plain words and it suggests matching
codes with their export policy. Always treat the suggestion as a starting point — confirm the
final 8-digit code with your customs house agent (CHA) or freight forwarder before you file your
first shipping bill, and re-confirm it if DGFT publishes a new ITC-HS edition.

*This page is general information, not customs or legal advice. Export policy conditions change;
always check the current DGFT schedule before you ship.*
