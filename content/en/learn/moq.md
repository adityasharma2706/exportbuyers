## What MOQ means

Your **Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ)** is the smallest amount of product you are willing to
produce and ship in a single order. It exists because most production runs have fixed costs —
machine setup, dyeing or printing batches, minimum fabric or raw-material lot sizes — that only
make sense to absorb above a certain quantity.

## Why stating it upfront helps

Buyers, especially larger ones, will ask for it early. If you state your MOQ clearly in your
outreach email and on your profile, you save time on both sides: buyers who cannot meet it will
say so quickly instead of going back and forth, and buyers who can meet it know you are set up to
produce at a real commercial scale.

## Setting a realistic MOQ

- Base it on your actual minimum efficient batch, not a round number picked to sound impressive.
- It is fine to have different MOQs for different products, colours or customisation levels — a
  standard catalogue item usually supports a lower MOQ than a fully custom design.
- Consider a lower "sample order" tier (see the guide on handling sample requests) that is
  separate from your bulk MOQ, so a serious buyer can test your quality before committing to a
  full order.

## Negotiating around MOQ

A buyer asking for a quantity below your MOQ is not automatically a lost lead. You can offer a
"first order" exception at a slightly higher unit price, a mixed-SKU order that reaches your MOQ
in total volume even if no single item does, or simply explain the cost reasons behind your MOQ —
buyers who plan to be repeat customers usually understand.

*This page is general guidance, not a substitute for your own costing analysis.*
