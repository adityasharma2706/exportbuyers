## The pattern

The person contacting you writes only from a free personal email provider (Gmail, Yahoo,
Outlook.com and similar) rather than an address on their company's own domain, even though they
claim to represent an established business with its own website.

## Why it matters, and why it is not proof by itself

A free-mail address is one of the weaker signals on its own — plenty of genuine small buyers,
individual traders and buying agents legitimately use personal email for business, especially in
smaller markets or at an early stage of a relationship. It becomes a meaningfully stronger signal
when it is combined with others: no company website at all, a website that does not match the
stated company name, or any of the other patterns in this guide.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask for the company's website and cross-check that the company name, product category and
  country make sense together.
- Where a website exists, ask whether they have a company email address you could also use — a
  reasonable request that a genuine business can usually accommodate.
- Treat a free-mail address as one data point, not a verdict, and weigh it alongside everything
  else you know about the buyer.
- Run the company, email and website through **Check a buyer**, which checks this alongside
  several other signals together rather than in isolation.
