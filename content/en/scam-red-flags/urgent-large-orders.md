## The pattern

A new contact, often one you have exchanged very few messages with, proposes an unusually large
order and pushes for a fast decision — language like "urgent", "today only", "ship immediately"
or "act now" paired with a large quantity or a large stated value.

## Why it works

Urgency is a classic pressure tactic: it is designed to get you to skip the checks you would
normally do (verifying the company, agreeing clear terms, running a background check) because
slowing down feels like it risks losing a big order. A genuine large buyer, by contrast, usually
has a longer sales process, not a shorter one — bigger commitments normally come with more
diligence, not less.

## How to protect yourself

- Treat urgency itself as a signal to slow down, not speed up. A real large order will still be
  there after you have done basic checks.
- Ask the questions you would normally ask for a large order regardless of the stated deadline:
  payment terms, Incoterm, product specification, and how they found you.
- Be especially cautious if the urgency is paired with an advance-fee request, a very new contact,
  or any other pattern in this guide — the combination is a much stronger signal than either alone.
- Run the company through **Check a buyer** before you commit to anything, however large the
  opportunity looks.
