## The pattern

The company name given to you and the domain of their website (or email address) do not
resemble each other at all — for example a company calling itself "Global Star Traders" writing
from a website domain with an entirely unrelated name.

## Why it matters, and why it is not proof by itself

Genuine businesses sometimes trade under a name different from their registered legal name, use a
shortened or abbreviated domain, or operate multiple brands from one company. A mismatch alone
does not prove a problem. But it is also a pattern seen when a scam operation borrows or
impersonates a real company's name while using a website and contact details that have nothing to
do with that company.

## How to protect yourself

- Search the company name independently and see whether the website you were given is the one
  that comes up, or whether a different, more established website exists for that name.
- Ask directly why the name and domain differ — a genuine business usually has a simple,
  reasonable explanation and will not mind being asked.
- If the mismatch is combined with other patterns in this guide (a fee request, a very new
  domain, urgency), treat it as a stronger warning.
- Run the company through **Check a buyer**, which checks name-and-domain similarity alongside
  other signals.
