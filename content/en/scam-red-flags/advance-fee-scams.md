## The pattern

A "buyer" agrees to place an order, sometimes a large one, and then explains that before the
order can proceed, you need to pay a fee — described as a "registration fee", "processing fee",
"customs clearance fee", "licence fee" or similar. The fee is usually much smaller than the value
of the supposed order, which is exactly why it looks reasonable at first.

There is no order. The fee, once paid, is the entire transaction, and the buyer disappears
afterwards, sometimes asking for one more "final" fee first.

## Why it works

Framing the request as a fee tied to *your* export process, rather than a payment to *them*,
makes it sound like normal red tape rather than a request for money. Real Indian export
processes do have genuine costs (courier, some documentation), but none of them are paid to the
buyer, and none of them are a precondition set by the buyer for the deal to continue.

## How to protect yourself

- No genuine buyer needs you to pay them, or an agency they nominate, before they can complete
  their side of a purchase. Payment flows from buyer to seller, not the other way round.
- If a fee is genuinely required for your own export process (a certificate you need, for
  example), you choose and pay the provider yourself — it is never dictated by the buyer, and
  never paid directly to them or an account they specify.
- Run the company through **Check a buyer** before you send any payment, and treat any advance
  payment request from an unverified new contact as a strong reason to stop and check further.
