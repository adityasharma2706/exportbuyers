Most enquiries you receive will be from real buyers. A small number will not be, and the ones
that aren't tend to follow recognisable patterns. Knowing these patterns — and running
**Check a buyer** whenever something feels off — is your best protection, especially before you
ship a sample or agree on payment terms with someone new.

## The seven patterns

1. **[Advance-fee or registration-fee requests](/scam-red-flags/advance-fee-scams)** — being
   asked to pay a "registration", "processing" or "clearance" fee before an order proceeds.
2. **[Certification-fee traps](/scam-red-flags/certification-fee-scams)** — being told to pay a
   named "certification" or "inspection" agency before the deal can continue.
3. **[Free-mail contact addresses](/scam-red-flags/freemail-contacts)** — a "company" contacting
   you only from a free personal email address, with no company domain in sight.
4. **[Newly registered domains](/scam-red-flags/new-domains)** — a company website registered
   only very recently, despite claims of being an established business.
5. **[Company name and website mismatch](/scam-red-flags/name-domain-mismatch)** — the company
   name and the website domain do not resemble each other at all.
6. **[Urgent, large orders](/scam-red-flags/urgent-large-orders)** — pressure to move fast on an
   unusually large order, often with unrealistic timelines.
7. **[Free-sample-only requests](/scam-red-flags/sample-only-requests)** — a request for free
   samples with no discussion of an order at all.

None of these on its own proves a scam — plenty of genuine buyers use a personal email address or
place a first, smaller order quickly. But when you see two or more of these together, or one very
strong signal like an upfront fee request, treat it as a serious warning.
