## The pattern

The company's website domain was registered very recently — sometimes only days or weeks before
they contacted you — despite the buyer describing themselves as an established, long-running
business.

## Why it matters, and why it is not proof by itself

A brand-new domain is not unusual for a genuinely new business, a company launching a new brand
or subsidiary, or one that recently moved to a new domain. But it directly contradicts a claim of
being long-established, and scam operations frequently spin up a fresh, simple website to look
credible for a short campaign before it gets shut down or abandoned.

## How to protect yourself

- If a buyer emphasises how long they have been in business, that claim should roughly match how
  long their website has existed. A significant mismatch is worth asking about directly.
- Look for other signs of an established business: a track record of shipments in trade data,
  reviews or mentions elsewhere, and consistent contact details across channels.
- Treat domain age as one data point, not a verdict, and weigh it alongside everything else you
  know about the buyer.
- Run the company through **Check a buyer**, which factors domain age in alongside other signals.
