## The pattern

A message asks for free samples — sometimes from several products at once — with no discussion at
all of an order: no target quantity, no target price, no timeline, and often no real questions
about your product either.

## Why it matters, and why it is not proof by itself

Asking for a sample is completely normal in export sourcing (see the
[guide to handling sample requests](/learn/sample-requests)) — the warning sign here is
specifically the *absence* of any order context around the request. A genuine buyer evaluating a
new supplier can usually answer at least one basic sourcing question (roughly how much they might
order, or by when); a request that is only ever about getting free product, sent to many suppliers
at once, usually cannot.

## How to protect yourself

- Ask a few qualifying questions before agreeing to send a sample: target quantity, target price
  range, and rough timeline. A genuine buyer can usually answer at least one.
- Where practical, ask the requester to use their own courier account or cover the courier cost —
  standard practice that a genuine buyer will not be surprised by.
- Keep sample quantities small regardless of who is asking.
- Run the company through **Check a buyer** before you send anything, especially if this pattern
  is combined with a free-mail address or a very new website.
