## The pattern

A close cousin of the advance-fee scam. Instead of asking you to pay them directly, the "buyer"
names a specific "certification agency", "inspection agency" or "quality certification body" and
tells you that you must pay that agency before the order can proceed. The agency is often
unfamiliar, sometimes with a name designed to sound like a real, well-known certification body.

## Why it is convincing

Real international trade does involve legitimate third-party inspection and certification —
pre-shipment inspection agencies, quality certifications for specific product categories, and so
on. Scammers exploit this by naming a fake or complicit "agency" and directing payment through
it, making the request sound like standard trade practice rather than a request for money to a
stranger.

## How to protect yourself

- If a buyer insists on a *specific* inspection or certification agency you have never dealt
  with, and payment goes through that agency before any product changes hands, treat it as a
  serious warning sign — especially if you cannot independently verify the agency exists and is
  reputable, separate from what the buyer tells you.
- Legitimate pre-shipment inspection is normally something *you* arrange (or agree with the buyer
  to use a mutually recognised, independently verifiable inspection company), not something the
  buyer mandates through an unfamiliar, hard-to-verify name.
- Search for the named agency independently — not through any link or contact detail the buyer
  gave you — before paying anything.
- Run the company through **Check a buyer** before you commit to any payment.
